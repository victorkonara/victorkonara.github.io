# Of the City of Daub, and the Manner of Its Governance

Your Grace will forgive me for what I am about to write of Daub. I do this not because I know better than you, but because I believe you should know what Daub looks from the outside.

When I first came to Daub, what I beheld was less a city (as I am used to) and more some strange lawless wilderness that should not have lasted a fortnight. Perhaps lawless is the wrong word; perhaps the problem is that there were too many laws, enforced by too many different people, and both people and law changed often, sometimes from month to month, sometimes from street to street. There were Charter Knights at the gate who would make a token attempt to stop mugging and murder - but only if it happened near their posts, barracks or daily routes. There was the Haugris Church, but for every priest I counted ten guards; often acolytes with sticks, but sometimes more.

There more I learned about the city the more confused I became. The inn I first stayed at, and its surroundings, were said to 'belong' to Charlie Two-Fingers. Who was Charlie Two-Fingers? I had no-idea, but the innkeep assured me that I was lucky not to know. There was Doris the Delightful, who  apparently ran or owned or collected protection money from half the bakeries in the city. This green field, surrounded by expensive cottages? All held by various Pendraigs or the poorer cousins of Wattle nobility: Haaslings, Caravels, Epponion, Bautista. There were Umber Knives, Pacino's Peacekeepers, the Paladins, the Playing Cards - all presumably reporting to Your Grace - but to an outside the only thing these people all had in common was that anything they did not like in their territory would get one beaten, robbed or outright murdered.  Crossing the city from one end another meant walking through a patchwork quilt of jurisdictions, a mosaic carved out by two-bit gangsters and nobles. 

It was common practice for a family with multiple grown children to send one to train with Charter Knights, the other to some merchant house, the other to be a ganster's lackey, and the other to look like a stuffed frog in some noble's pay - that was the only way to know which way the winds of Fortune  would blow tomorrow. The only people nobody seemed to bother were the bankers and the Merchant's Guild, probably because they were ones handling payroll. There are courts, but who answers their summons? Only nobles whose houses prefer to lose face or show off some expensive rhetoric than shell out the gold for another turf war. The Lesser Theoretical University had its own guards (mercenaries) and half the staff were old Singhapura war mages prowling for anyone stupid enough to break in. 

I do not criticize Your Grace for this approach; no doubt it worked. But let me tell you what Singhapura sees: 

Daub sits next to the river Sut. The old ruins of Himakatupura provided some ground; every subsequent generation added to what the previous one began. Its position and its resources have turned this into one of the fastest-growing cities in the Empire. There is an supply of labor, ever drawn from the youth of the provinces, who come to the city to make their fortune. There is iron, tin, lead, coal, lumber, wool, grain, cattle, coffee, tea. There is the  Waste, which provides the Merchants Guild with rare magical vessels and Relics. 

And this whole thing is protected by outer walls that a confused flock of sheep could leap over and is run by, depending on who you ask, a motley assembly of two-bit crooks, aristrocrats, wheelers, dealers, assasins and prostitutes. It is common knowledge in the Empire that Daub is kept the way it is because of the profitability of  have a place where the Empire can conduct its more unsavory business - especially with other powers of the world -  and because Daub can always be conquered in a weekend if the Adhiraj was bored enough.


## Of the Balance of Powers Within Daub

I shall not attempt to decipher the structure of the gangs or merchants of Daub. However, drawing from my studies of power, allow me to bring the lamplight closer to the structures that I think most likely to prevail in the long-term, even if their commanding personnel were to dissapear. The key to long-lasting power is stability. The key to stability is a unified set of consequences for actions - what we call rule of law - and institutions.

The first institution are the Charter Knights. Singhapura developed the Charter Knights because old soldiers, whose glory days are behind them, can still be of some use - and because, robbed of purpose, they tend to get up to mischief. There will always be Charter Knights; as long as there are violent people who are content to make a simpler living, there will be plenty of people happy to put on some armour, run a few patrols, and apprehend a troublemaker or two.  

The Charter Knights give Your Grace direct enforcement and act as the most authoritative arm of your justice. The question is what they are supposed to do other than guard the gates. Given the nature of Your Grace's conquest of Daub, it is natural that you do not fully trust the Charter Knights. But this is a blade that must either be reforged or disbanded altogether. Daub's Charter Knights, Your Grace, need some purpose; currently they are little more than people paid from your own coffers to stand around and do nothing while Daub falls to a million small sins. I say nothing of Your Grace's own loyal men, but it is worth bearing in mind that resentment, envy, and complaint may arise in any body.

The Merchants Guild of Daub is the most interesting power in the city. Insofar as I can discern they are rather similar to the Singhapura Merchants guild in that hold no formal office. However, though they are mentioned in no bylaw, act or proclamation, their underwriters sit inside every significant transaction; their guards police their warehouses and assets; their suppliers and wagon trains pass through every district. Political advisors who have treated them as a footnote have been removed. 

This power is thus both structurally indispensable and a threat to your position. I do not advise Your Grace to destroy this arrangement; it cannot be done without destroying the city's commercial vitality; but nor should the Guild be allowed to mistake accommodation for dependence. Your Grace must find a purpose for them subordinate to your interests, or else you fall into the position of a tenant ruling over a landlord. Given that the Merchants possibly have more arms at their command than all others in Daub, it falls to Your Grace to find some mutually profitable endeavour that incentivize them to make sure Your Grace remains in power.  

In this Your Grace has a powerful ally; - the Tombulov estates, which hold a monopoly on the production of coffee. Their estates are well-defended; their product and their means to distribute it have spread far and wide across the land, from remote villages unto Singhapura itself; and they are, above all else, independent. They are perhaps the finest example I have encountered of Daub the fruits of the Empire  — roads, networks for messaging, law and accoutrements — to build a new network of power. I urge Your Grace to re-examine carefully these operations and to learn from them.

Additionally, without suggesting ovrmuch of the relationship between Your Grace and Lady Tombulov, the vast number of coffeehouses spread across the land may serve Your Grace as a powerful means of moving messages to and fro, if possible.

The third institution is the Lesser Theoretical University of Daub. As the only serious rival (and near-equivalent ally) to the Taprobane Imperial University, they are the only people in the city capable of certain classes of problem-solving. Access to them is a form of leverage. Presently they sit apart and outside the doings of the city at large. I would advise Your Grace to make use of them and to cultivate this relationship; it will not sustain itself indefinitely without attention.

The fourth institution is the nobility. Your Grace seems to have actively rescinded their power and replaced them with your own. However, in this, Your Grace is simply perpetuating an institution. A noble is nothing more than a legitimate thug in fine clothing. Charlie Two-Fingers may answer to Your Grace right now, but unless Your Grace repeatedly and publicly places all these elements beneath your power, it will only take a generation or two before a their descendants begin vying for power themselves.

The Singhapura method of maintaining control is for the state to maintain overwhelming authority and force. Governance is based on a monopoly on violence. Your Grace's methods, insofar as I can understand, is to counterbalance parties capable of violence against one another. The general battle of faith between the Haugris Church and the Temple of Eighteen is an example, as is Your Grace's support of the Paladins as an alternative to a potential Church monopoly on heavy enforcement. I will not criticize this arrangement - after all, the Lusians use it - but I will note that having many small groups, each with their own definition of lawful authority, takes a toll on those leading everyday lives. In the least, these groups should be unified on certain fundamental principles and in their support of Your Grace, even if they are at odds with one another.

Your Grace should also perhaps carefully examine which of these bodies would act first in a crisis, and portion tasks and oversight accordingly. Redudant capacity is a source of strength but not when mismanaged. 

Then there are those outside the city itself that still come under Your Grace's authority. Jerem of the Redhills, once known as Bloody Jerem or Jerem the Red; Carfax and Rouster to the north; Wyrmstead and Vacourt to the east, not to mention minor vassals such as the Ashwoods the Pondrawaters. I should point out here that while they answer to you, their legitimacy, and thus their rule, comes from King Allant of Wattle. In this the map resembles not so much Daub and its vassal states than Daub being surrounded on all sides by people who would be happy to displace Your Grace if King Allant were to give the order.

The providence of this situation is that Wattle is still distant, and it is up to Your Grace to tie these vassals to you in such a way that maintaining the status quo is more profitable for them than removing your head. 

## Of Daub's Position Within the Empire

While trade flourishes, Singhapura is not dependent on Daub. However, the arrangement Daub has - tribute, nominal deference, and the Empire's occasional high-handedness - is, if I may point out, startlingly lenient for a vassal state of Singhapura. It may be that the Adhiraj intends to let Daub flourish as a fruitful vassal on its north-easternmost border; it may also be the fruit is being ripened enough for the plucking. 

The Singhapura political theorist Rukarya wrote that Daub, lacking tradition to check its genius, is free and therefore dangerous. He meant this as a caution. Freedom of operation is both a rare advantage and a burden. A very soft noose, as they say. Your Grace should observe the Empire's movements in Wattle and Tangowan. 


## Further Reading

Rukarya's *Comparative Administrations* is the only serious political study that looks deeply at Daub, and Your Grace should definitely read it. It may give you many ideas for how to arrange the puzzle pieces that you currently posess. 

*Angenga* is a novel, and its author is unknown. However, but its portrait of the early years of Daub's Merchant Guild and the disposition of those factions is a work of careful observation, and shows how far and deep profit can take men. Your Grace may find in here certain histories that are only a name or two removed from the people you must work with. 

For the Paladins and their institutional origins, see Singhapura, Military Orders.
